The quiet art of a garden in fall

Autumn is when a garden shows its bones. The borders thin out, the light comes in low and gold, and every shape you planted in spring suddenly matters. This is the season to plant bulbs for May, to lift and divide the tired perennials, and to leave the seed heads standing for the birds. Mulch the beds before the first frost, sow sweet peas in deep pots, and move the tender plants under glass. Above all, take notes: what grew too tall, what never flowered, which corner stayed wet. A garden is a long conversation, and autumn is when it listens. Rake the leaves into the borders rather than the bin; by spring the worms will have pulled them into the soil. Cut the dahlias back once the first frost blackens them, lift the tubers, and store them dry in a box of sawdust. Plant tulips last of all, in November, a hand’s depth down, in groups of seven or nine rather than in rows. Order bare-root roses now: they cost less than potted ones and settle in faster. The cold does useful work too. It breaks up heavy clay, thins out the pests, and tells the fruit trees it is time to rest. Leave one corner wild, with a log pile and a heap of hollow stems, and there will be hedgehogs by March. Clean the pots, oil the tools, and empty the water butts before the pipes can freeze. Then sit down with a cup of tea and the seed catalogues, and plan the year you would like to have. Next issue: winter pruning, the case for a pond, and the best apple trees for small gardens.

Opening Folio the first time

The installers aren’t code-signed yet, so your computer asks you once to confirm.

On Windows

  1. Open the installer you downloaded.
  2. If “Windows protected your PC” appears, choose More info.
  3. Choose Run anyway.

On a Mac

  1. Open the .dmg and drag Folio into Applications.
  2. Open Folio. If macOS stops it, open System Settings → Privacy & Security.
  3. Choose Open Anyway.

05Q&A

Questions, answered.

How do I activate Folio?

The first time Folio starts, it asks for your serial number. Paste it in and choose Activate — you only do this once on each computer.

Can it open Microsoft Publisher files?

Yes. Open a .pub file and Folio brings in its text and pictures, ready to lay out again. Publisher’s exact layout isn’t kept, and your work is saved as a .folio file.

Which computers does it run on?

Windows 11 and 10 (64-bit), and macOS 13 or later on both Apple silicon and Intel Macs.

Does it work offline?

Completely. Templates, fonts and all 85 spelling dictionaries are installed with the app.

Why does my computer warn me when I install it?

The installers aren’t code-signed yet. Follow the steps under the download tickets — you only need to allow it once.

How do I share my work?

Export a print-ready PDF or PNG/JPEG images, or print directly. Publications are saved as .folio files.

How do I update?

Download the latest installer from this page and run it — your publications and settings are kept.
